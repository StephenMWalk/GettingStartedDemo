    Section Calculus II handout: "Some Calculus II problems" (Regions between curves)--a start

    Example 1.

    Find the area of the finite region between the graphs of \(y = 16 - x^{2}\) and \(y = x + 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We check for intersection points:
    \begin{align*} 16 - x^2 \amp = x + 4, \hbox{so} \\ 0 \amp = x^2 + x - 12, \hbox{so that} \\ 0 \amp = (x + 4) \cdot (x - 3). \end{align*}
    Then \(x = -4\) and \(x = 3\) are the solutions to the equation and, hence, the \(x\)-coordinates of the intersection points of the graphs.
    🔗
    The area is then given by
    \begin{align*} \int_{-4}^{3}\ ((16 - x^2) - (x + 4))\,dx \amp = \int_{-4}^{3}\ (-x^2 - x + 12)\,dx \\ \amp = \left.\left(-\frac{1}{3}x^3 - \frac{1}{2}x^2 + 12x \right)\right|_{-4}^3 \\ \amp = \left(\frac{45}{2}\right) - \left(-\frac{104}{3}\right) \\ \amp = \frac{135}{6} + \frac{208}{6} \\ \amp = \frac{343}{6} \\ \amp = 57\frac{1}{6}. \end{align*}
